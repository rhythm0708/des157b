Project Overview

For my capstone project, I designed a website exploring how the future might be shaped by artifical intelligence. I chose this topic because it is timely and deeply consequential to our future. I imagine the site as a safe, speculative space where users can reflect on and engage with potential AI-driven futures. While targeted towards teenagers and young adults, it is designed to be accessible to people of all ages.

My goal for this project was to move beyond the simple games and interactive experiments that I had been creating throughout the year and challenge myself with an informational website. Through this project, I hoped to develop my visual design, animation, and data visualization skills to tell a compelling story about a topic that affects everyone.

The structure of my project is simple. The homepage is a carousel which allows users to browse and select articles based on title and caption. Clicking on an article expands it to reveal a long, scrollable page in front of a cloud background. Articles are designed with generous spacing between sections to give users space to pause and think before continuing. Each article is a short discussion / commentary about an AI concept that may be shaping our future world.

Project Research

To create believable, true-to-life narratives, I conducted research into how AI is being applied to different domains. First, I conducted informal user research through conversations with classmates, friends, and professor Mead. These conversations helped me identify emerging trends and areas of interest worth exploring further. Then, I followed up with online research on YouTube and reputable news sites such as Forbes and Medium.

I also conducted comparative research into similar web-based projects and noticed that many lacked depth, often being based in the author's personal perspective. As a result, I felt that a lot of these projects lacked value or informative-ness. Here, I saw an opportunity to differentiate myself by grounding my speculative narratives in real-world examples and supporting them with data. This approach allows users to feel like they are learning and reflecting on something meaningful while engaging with my project.

Visual Design

I established the visual design early in the project and developed it further when doing mid-fis and hi-fis. I aimed for a dreamy, ethereal atmosphere—one that evoked a feeling of safety and reflection as users pondered their thoughts on AI. I focused on using animation and color to create smooth, seamless, and immersive transitions between the homepage and each article.

I initially struggled with creating radial gradients that transitioned well against each other without any harsh color breaks. I learned that while many gradients looked good on their own, I had to closely watch how the gradients transitioned to ensure a seamless animation. Another thing I learned was to select more highlight and accent colors in my color scheme. I had forgotten to consider hover and clicked states when doing my mid- and hi-fis.

User Interface

My goal for the user interface was to keep it simple and intuitive. I aimed to minimize the number of actions available to the user at a time, while maximizing the number of ways to perform each action. This approach is most evident in the carousel on my homescreen, which users can navigate through by swiping, clicking on the arrows, or using the keyboard (WIP). To create this functionality, I used the Swiper JavaScript library and built CSS/JS on top of it.

Within the articles, I made sure to abide by basic principles of readability and cognitive load. I kept each line between 6–12 words, and chunked each article into distinct, digestable sections. This improves comprehension but also gives space for users to reflect before moving onto the next idea. If the text had been presented together, users might skim through without fully engaging with the content.

The simplicity of my website allowed most testers to navigate it intuitively without much difficulty. That said, during the final showcase, a few users didn't realize they had to scroll down to continue reading (due to the large gap between sections). To remedy this, I could add overlay text saying "Scroll down to keep reading" that disappears once the user starts scrolling. Another helpful feature could be a progress bar. But overall, users were able to complete their tasks easily and successfully, examples of which are:

  1. Navigate into any article.
  2. Return to the homepage after clicking into any article.
  3. Read the article about AI in recycling.
  4. Return to the home page by clicking 'X' on any article.

Revisions

Usability testing was another important step that helped me identify key issues and refine the concept/content of my project. My tests revealed that users liked the simple and intuitive navigation of my site, but felt that the writing could be more speculative. At this point, I had mostly been describing present-day AI practices while users had hoped to learn about unseen or lesser-known AI futures. This early realization helped me revise the approach of my project to work towards a more imaginative and impactful final experience.

My tests also revealed the importance of visuals. Although they had not yet been implemented, their absence made it clear that they were essential to the impact of the articles. Here, I asked testers whether they would prefer to see sketches, vector graphics, or real photographs in the placeholder spots. Most felt that sketches would best fit the dreamy, ethereal tone of the site—photographs felt too grounded in reality. This feedback motivated me to spend more energy developing the visuals of my project, which would help bring it to life.

Summary

All in all, I'm proud of the final outcome and learned a lot throughout the process. Given time limitations and other constraints I faced this quarter, I think that keeping the scope small was a smart decision. I learned how to effectively prioritize, use user feedback to target key areas, and thoughtfully integrate sound, visuals, and native HTML/CSS elements into a project.

What I'm most proud of in this project are the animations and transitions between the homepage and articles. I thought they were creative and visually appealing. I'm also pretty happy with the mood I was able to create through visuals, color, and music—it came surprisingly close to my original vision. Finally, it was rewarding to learn to work with the Swiper library and customize it beyond its default capabilities.

The most challenging part of this process were the drawings and managing the HTML. Due to the length of the articles, the HTML became unwieldy and difficult to navigate. In the future, I hope to look for an easier and more streamlined way to handle long files. I also hope to continue improving my digital drawing skills so I can create higher-quality visuals more quickly.
