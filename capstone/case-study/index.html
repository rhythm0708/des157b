Project Overview

For my capstone project, I designed a website exploring how the future might be shaped by artifical intelligence. I chose this topic because it is timely and deeply consequential to our future. I imagine the site as a safe, speculative space where users can reflect on and engage with potential AI-driven futures. While targeted towards teenagers and young adults, it is designed to be accessible to people of all ages.

My goal for this project was to move beyond the simple games and interactive experiments that I had been creating throughout the year and challenge myself with an informational website. Through this project, I hoped to develop my visual design, animation, and data visualization skills to tell a compelling story about a topic that affects everyone.

The structure of my project is simple. The homepage is a carousel which allows users to browse and select articles based on title and caption. Clicking on an article expands it to reveal a long, scrollable page in front of a cloud background. Articles are designed with generous spacing between sections to give users space to pause and think before continuing. Each article is a short discussion / commentary about an AI concept that may be shaping our future world.

Project Research

To inform my project, it was important that I complete in-depth research about AI to propose feasible speculative narratives. If my ideas are too far-fetched, users might not buy in. I made sure to look to reputable news sources and articles from Forbes, Medium, and _ to understand the wider impact of AI.

I also conducted comparative research and looked at other websites on similar topics. I found that a lot of websites lacked the depth of research or compelling narrative to carry user interest. They mainly presented ideas with little context. I would differentiate myself my doing this and adding depth to the speculation to produce a more immersive experience.

Visual Design

I settled on the visual design early on in my project, and developed it slightly while creating mid/hi-fis. I wanted to work with dynamic gradients, transitions, and simple fade aniamtions on my project.

One challenge that I faced developing the visual design was designing a dynamic radial gradient that did not have clear breaks and animates smoothly. I found that although many gradients looked good individually, they did not transition well against each other. I also received comments about the sudden fade-out after an article was closed. I fixed that in future iterations.

User Interface

I aimed to create a smooth user interface, similar to an in-game menu where you can select between items. I attempted to create it in vanilla JS, but I ultimately found it easier to work with a library, Swiper, which would simultaneously allow the website to be touch (mobile-) friendly. By working with the variables and animations already provided by Swiper and building on top of it, I was able to come up with this.

Some things I made sure to consider in my design was readability and consistency. I wanted to make sure that elements appeared in a logical, predictable, and consistent manner so users could focus on the content. I also used chunking strategies to make my articles easy to read: I limited words to 10 per line and put images + gaps between sentences to allow time for users to absorb and create an easy reading experience.

This allowed me to create a fluid website experience which was mostly intuitive for my testers. Users knew how to navigate my website without much hassle and could perform tasks without asking questions, exploring the website freely. Some examples of tasks on my website are:

  1. Click into any article.
  2. Read the article about AI in recycling.
  3. Return to the home page after fully reading an article.
  4. Return to the home page by clicking the 'X' button on anonymous article.

Revisions

I conducted usability testing to identify any major usability issues in my project. Overall, users found my project simple but very intuitive. They liked tha transitions and overall theme, but thought that the writing could be more obviously speculative. I was mostly describing existing practices that I had found through my research, but had room to extend. I worked on improving this in the latter stretch of my project.

Another thing that I worked on was the visuals. For the usability test, I used placeholder images but I was still debating on whether to do sketches, vector graphics, or real images for the real thing. When using real images, I was concerned about copyright so I was moreso leaning towards drawn vectors. After being hinted by playtesters to focus on that, I went forward and put more energy into that to bring life to my articles.

Summary

In summary, I am content with the results of my capstone project and learned a lot through the process of making it. Beyond creating digital drawings and writing about new content, I got good practice using libraries and animating cool transitions for my website and ended up with something pretty cool.

In this project, I am most proud of the scope. Most of the other websites I created for these classes were simple, short games with direct functionality. This is shockingly the first informational website I've made since Interactive Media I and the blog. I'm proud that I was able to put together so much information and structure for my page. Finally, I'm proud that it is easily expandable and that it wouldn't take much effort to simply add another article.

As previously mentioned, the most challenging part of this process was creating the drawings but also the tedious process of managing the HTML. Due to the length of the articles, the HTML ended up over 400 lines long and the CSS was similar. Navigating both of these became tedious and long. I would like to find a simpler way to condense these in the future. The most rewarding aspect was getting the dreamy atmosphere that I wanted. I never really emphasized visual UI design in any of my other projects and I'm glad I got the chance to here.
